Eris is a dwarf planet and the most massive known member of the scattered disc — a distant population of icy bodies beyond the Kuiper Belt. Discovered in January 2005 by a team led by Mike Brown at Palomar Observatory, Eris is slightly smaller than Pluto in diameter but about 27% more massive, making it the most massive dwarf planet. Its discovery created a crisis in planetary science: if Pluto was a planet, then so was Eris — but that opened the door to dozens more "planets" in the outer solar system. The controversy led directly to the 2006 IAU resolution that demoted Pluto and created the dwarf planet category. Eris's highly reflective surface is thought to be covered in a thin layer of frozen nitrogen and methane that condenses out of its atmosphere as it moves to the far reaches of its orbit.
