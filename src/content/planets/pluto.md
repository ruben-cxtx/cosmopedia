Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt, a region of the solar system beyond the orbit of Neptune. Discovered in 1930, it was considered the ninth planet for 76 years until the International Astronomical Union redefined planet classification in 2006. Despite its small size, Pluto is a scientifically fascinating world. The 2015 New Horizons flyby shattered expectations, revealing a geologically young and active surface with towering mountains of water ice, vast nitrogen glaciers, and a thin but surprisingly complex atmosphere. Pluto and its largest moon Charon form a binary system unlike any other in the solar system, with both bodies tidally locked, always showing the same face to each other.
